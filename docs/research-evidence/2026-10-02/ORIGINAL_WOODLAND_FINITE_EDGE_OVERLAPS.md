# Woodland finite edge overlap follow-up

The new [source-only overlap inventory](interior-source-overlaps/finite-edge-overlaps.json) extends the [full-edge junction inventory](ORIGINAL_WOODLAND_SOURCE_JUNCTION_INVENTORY.md). Its [generator](../../../scripts/research/inventory-original-shed-edge-overlaps.py) retains all 196 original triangles, 415 exact positions, original first-channel UVs and all 588 edge bindings. Face 62 remains present with null plane identity because it is exactly degenerate. No source geometry is edited, welded or replaced.

Every distinct-face edge pair within each component is tested: 42,777 pairs in A and 42,777 in B. The finite interval test requires positive projected overlap and both endpoints of each edge within 0.002 source units of the opposite infinite line. Each accepted diagnostic retains overlap lengths, angular error, both line distances and admission at 0.00001, 0.0001, 0.001 and 0.002 source-unit allowances. Exact endpoints remain untouched; interpolated positions, world coordinates and raw UVs at overlap limits are derived diagnostics, not replacement geometry or image correspondences.

A retains 180 binding-pair incidences, including 14 previously ungrouped or partial overlaps; B retains 174, including eight previously ungrouped pairs. All 14 new A overlaps exceed the maximum distance allowance in length and survive the strictest 0.00001 allowance. They comprise wall/horizontal, coplanar wall, horizontal/horizontal and degenerate-face incidences. All eight new B overlaps are shorter than 0.002 source units and are explicitly classified as numerical endpoint slivers, rather than finite boundary support. Four survive 0.00001 and all eight survive 0.0001. Every result remains recorded.

No newly recovered overlap is a wall/sloped-plane incidence. The 30 A and 33 B wall/sloped binding-pair incidences already belong to full-edge groups in the prior inventory. Counts here refer to binding pairs, while the earlier 24 A and 27 B counts refer to grouped edges; they are not contradictory. This bounded edge-edge search therefore supplies no missing roof/wall boundary that resolves the earlier component asymmetry or identifies an interior image corner.

The test covers near-collinear finite edge pairs, including positive subdivision overlap. It does not cover a vertex touching the interior of a face, arbitrary triangle intersections, or an edge ending exactly at another endpoint. A tolerance admission is a numerical diagnostic and does not establish native welded topology. Nearly parallel distinct surfaces can satisfy the allowance. The absence of a new incidence is consequently not proof of a hole or missing roof geometry.

All image roles remain unchanged: apparent 11/12-second room junctions are exploratory, photographed rails and the bright roof feature are appearance only, and no source component is assigned to an image. New fit and heldout sets are empty. A useful next source-only step is a vertex/edge-in-face or plane-intersection examination of the historically compatible interior wall tops, followed by independent image boundary identification if an actual finite intersection exists. No fit, browser, original runtime, native illumination or V12/V14 acceptance is established. Production, progress and earlier manifests are unchanged.
