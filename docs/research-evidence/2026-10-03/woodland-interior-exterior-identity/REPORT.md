# Cafe panel identity outside the native interior opening

The authoritative unmodified 22.3-second [native frame](../woodland-interior-endpoints/frame-05.png), the recovered cafe atlas, and both new nearest-neighbor inspection crops were actually viewed. No contrast, lighting, camera, production or test settings changed. This investigates an exterior constraint independently of the dark barn floor seam: the pale wall visible through the opening has **two narrow barred windows**, matching the distinctive upper-right cafe atlas panel. The dark stain at the upper edge and window spacing also agree qualitatively. This identifies a useful cafe *panel* hypothesis more specifically than a general building-class match; it is not a calibrated descriptor or pixel error measurement.

[Native cafe inspection](cafe-inspection.png), [unaltered atlas-panel inspection](atlas-panel-inspection.png), [full source inventory](geometry.json), [UV triangle overlay](panel-uv.svg).

The source inventory retains all 302 cafe faces and all local/world vertex positions. Exactly nine faces have their entire adapted UV support inside the predeclared inspected atlas box x349..512/y126..221. Three large exterior coplanar faces **36/44/93** cover the full-height two-window panel, with local outward normal approximately (-1,0,0). The others reuse this atlas region on an opposite-facing narrow strip, an end return, and horizontal top surfaces (94/102/119/125/136/167). Thus atlas identity alone is not a unique triangle identity. The visible full-height window-bearing wall is consistent with 36/44/93, while the narrow opposite-facing strip 94/102 cannot itself explain the displayed full window heights.

Both windows are painted within those three triangles. Their visible corners and bars are **not independent recovered mesh vertices**. Choosing four image window corners would therefore invent four geometric anchors. UV landmarks could support a separately qualified textured-surface registration, but this analysis has not recovered the native UV sampling or independently calibrated their pixel positions.

Two finite panel-top junction candidates are visible near native (980,246) and (1107,246); conservatively inspect boxes [975,241,985,251] and [1102,241,1112,251]. The source panel top endpoints are cafe vertices **143 and 178**. Their source-world positions are respectively (2322.1633399853563,-1627.512676134742,-282.6154964338855) and (2288.853483482249,-2020.2371064863974,-282.6154964338855). Vertex143 also meets lower endpoint195 at the same local Y; vertex178 shares position with end-return vertex177. These are exact recovered positions, **not admitted native pixel correspondences**. Texture/geometry panel identity remains a hypothesis, and assigning the two endpoint directions assumes ordinary unreflected projection and an interior camera location that has not independently been recovered. The left meeting also adjoins another pale wall rather than being a clean sky silhouette. Neither conservative inspection box meets a two-pixel localization gate.

The orange roof does not supply an independent finite apex: its high/right extent runs behind the barn's image-right jamb. Its visible sloping lower edge meets/overlaps the pale panel top near (1055,245), which need not be an actual source vertex; it can be a projected occlusion crossing. Promoting that intersection to a roof corner would choose geometry by appearance without proving the endpoint's identity.

[Source XY plan](source-plan.svg) and [finite ray samples](provenance.json) preserve a separate necessary visibility check. Rays from each of the four source room corners and their footprint centroid to each cafe panel top endpoint are intersected with the recovered barn gap line. These are ten finite samples, **not a camera search or an exhaustive impossibility result**. The centroid's rays cross beyond gap B (gap fractions >1); other camera positions need not do so. Only source-XY crossing is tested: ray heights, camera eye height and other occluders remain untested. The samples therefore cannot establish native visibility or reject all interior camera positions. They concretely show why building texture identity does not by itself settle the native opening/camera identity.

**Result:** retain a stronger, independent exterior panel hypothesis and two explicitly conditional panel-top endpoint candidates. No source anchors are admitted, no training/heldout roles are assigned, and no camera fit or native two-pixel registration claim is made. V12/V14 remain open. The next useful registration evidence would establish the panel/gap visibility from a verified viewpoint and independently identify a finite roof endpoint or another architectural edge, rather than count painted window corners as mesh vertices.
