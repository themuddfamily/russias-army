# AK front-sight topology and visibility candidates

The new [passive topology evidence](ak-front-sight-topology/front-sight-topology.json), generated by [this script](../../../scripts/research/audit-original-ak-front-sight-topology.py), binds the existing AK camera-local inventory, original weapon geometry and preserved 12-second image by hash. It retains all 814 exact vertices, 359 finite triangles and original first-channel UVs. No exactly degenerate triangles were found; 206 vertices are unused by the triangle list. Candidate selection alone therefore cannot establish a rendered endpoint.

The existing front-sight crown and stem coordinate gates touch 31 triangles. These remain coordinate-based hypotheses, not an authored part label. Every finite incident edge retains source vertex IDs, exact coordinates, raw UVs, adjacent faces and conditional boundary status. The crown's 12 selected vertex IDs occupy only three exact-coordinate groups: 42,44,153. Crown IDs 172,342,349 are unused; stem IDs 85,170,172,174,333,339,342,349 are unused. Used duplicates can share their coordinates, but an unused ID is not silently promoted into a rendered landmark.

Source-index fragmentation reflects duplicate seam vertices and does not establish physical gaps. The exact-coordinate model has 207 groups; the near-coordinate diagnostic has 199. Neither graph modifies positions, indices or UVs, and near grouping is not native welding proof.

Under both coordinate grouping models the selected region belongs to a 68-face component with 34 grouped vertices and 102 edges: Euler characteristic zero, with two distinct face incidences at every edge. This is a concrete candidate for closed handle-like sight topology under the grouping assumption. Manifold vertex links, orientability, geometric embedding and the physical direction of any opening are not established by these counts. They cannot alone identify a visible sight hole in the recording. The 31-face selected patch has one degree-two boundary cycle with 15 grouped vertices; all 54 selected incident edges have two global face incidences. Its cycle therefore bounds the coordinate-selected patch and is not an accepted global open rim.

In the preserved 1280×574 image, the approximate front-sight ROI (682,303,717,355) contains small dark post/stem morphology above the fore-end. The timber background, low contrast and recording raster obscure lower attachment and exact finite tips. No source endpoint, silhouette edge or hole rim is assigned to a pixel. Topology alone cannot prove which finite edge is front-facing or visible after self-occlusion. The JSON retains every candidate edge rather than selecting one by a hypothetical projection.

Both existing aspect hypotheses are carried forward unchanged: square output viewport with fx=fy≈615.473486 pixels, and an 800×600 camera stretched to 1280×574 with fx≈1029.363322 and fy≈615.473486. Native Euler order, inherited hither/near clipping, translation/recoil/loading recovery, velocity-dependent wave/bob, principal center/crop and recording version remain unknown. No synthetic projection, camera fit, browser or original runtime was run.

Image roles remain exploratory morphology only; accepted, fit and heldout correspondence sets are empty. A bounded next step is to check manifold/embedding and finite opening geometry of the retained 68-face source component, then inspect whether a particular finite rim/tip can be identified independently in higher-quality preserved frames. Separate receiver/fore-end geometry would be needed for validation, with roles fixed before a fit. V23 is not accepted. Production, progress and earlier manifests remain unchanged.
