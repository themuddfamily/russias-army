# Native interior opening endpoints in adjacent frames

Six exact decoded frames at 21.5, 21.7, 21.9, 22.1, 22.3 and 22.5 seconds were extracted from the preserved silent first-45-second Woodland excerpt. Each is 1280×574; no brightness, filtering or native pixel resizing was applied. All six were actually viewed. FFmpeg used one decoding and one encoding thread under the memory boundary. [Provenance](provenance.json), [decode log](extraction.log).

The adjacent frames resolve a local visibility limitation of the previous 22.0-second view. At **22.3 seconds**, the visible opening has two finite top corners: image-left near **(931,199)** and image-right near **(1140,149)**. The latter meets bright exterior sky, and its top boundary turns into a vertical wall silhouette. At **22.5 seconds**, the left top corner is near **(855,137)**, while the other jamb is outside the right image boundary. These are manually inspected architectural silhouette candidates, with deliberately conservative pixel boxes in provenance. They are not automated subpixel measurements or a claimed two-pixel localization.

[22.3s original](frame-05.png), [22.5s original](frame-06.png), [left-top inspection crops](top-endpoint-inspection.png), [22.3s right-top inspection crop](right-top-inspection.png). Crops are nearest-neighbor display derivatives without contrast changes; native originals are authoritative. The full frames and crops both show the finite top transition. Temporal consistency here means human inspection of neighboring views; no optical-flow or descriptor identity test was performed.

The source assignment remains **conditional** on this being the same recovered long-wall gap and the assumptions in the previous [identity report](../woodland-interior-identity/REPORT.md). Under that hypothesis, B top 198 is the image-left corner and A top 183 is the image-right corner. This investigation does not independently establish the gap identity, native camera pitch/roll, or screen handedness. Therefore the corner pixels are not admitted as exact source-vertex camera anchors. All four floor-corner alternatives 178,193,186,224 remain recorded; corner224 is only the prior favored hypothesis.

At 22.3s the lower left opening boundary runs into a dark floor/weapon region, and the lower right endpoint is below the image. At 22.5s the weapon obscures the lower left region, and the counterpart jamb remains outside the frame. The far floor seam changes direction near (199,354) at 22.5s, but its vertical wall meeting is dark and not independently separable from material/texture boundaries. It cannot be promoted to corner224. The cafe visible outside provides roof and facade outlines, but none was independently matched to an exact source vertex here; the tank is partly hidden in earlier frames and absent from the clearest corner frame. No four independently identified source anchors are obtained in a single frame, and no training/heldout partition is assigned.

This result supports a better native endpoint inspection input, not completed V12/V14 registration or a lighting adjustment. No camera fit, render, browser, original runtime or production edit was performed. No matcher was run, since a photometric track cannot resolve the source identity ambiguity. The first attempted derivative invocation via stdin produced no output because the transient service received no script; it exited successfully and is retained in the task history. It was corrected by using the preserved script file, without changing memory limits.
