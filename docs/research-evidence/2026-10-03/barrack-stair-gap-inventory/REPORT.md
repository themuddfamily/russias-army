# Barrack horizontal surfaces, stair candidates and strip obstruction

This investigation inventories the recovered fixed Barrack scene and tests concrete candidates against current shared player physics. It changes no production code. It does not prove historical/native equivalence, registered footage routes, complete stair coverage, or absence of every possible jump trajectory. V10 remains open.

`inventory.ts` uses the production stored-parent transform evaluator and source-Z-up to world-Y-up conversion. It retains 23,833 nondegenerate fixed-model triangles from the 95-node/93-mesh recovered scene, including their exact transformed vertices, oriented normals, source node, resource, part and face. The 1,269 horizontal faces form 383 shared-edge components. Horizontal bounds summarize actual triangles; they are not automatically filled support polygons. The analytical search compares 73,153 component pairs and finds 35 necessary-condition pairs with projected boundary separation below 1e-5, rise 0.05–0.6 world units, and common projected width greater than the player diameter (1.0117647058823529). These thresholds are inventory filters, not changed physical rules.

Crucially, horizontal alone does not mean support. `orientation.log` preserves each candidate's first-face oriented normal. Most apparent steps are downward-facing building undersides. The initially interesting low-level sequence in `bigredfellaopen` groups107/167/115 has heights 2.482/2.695/3.051 and looks like three narrow steps in bounds, but its faces point downward. `replay.ts` preserves the resulting failed interpretation: the actual starting support is floor at approximately zero, and the player traverses below the sequence. Both the ordinary movement and one original-impulse jump cross the projected interval in 12 callbacks without geometric intersection; neither is evidence of stair ascent.

The three pairs whose inspected first-face normals are exactly upward are isolated: groups80/118 (rise0.07124345), groups82/97 (rise0.42746349), and groups94/134 (rise0.21373731). There is no three-component upward chain among these reported necessary-condition pairs. Group291/301 has first normals approximately0.951714/1, and group277/301 has opposite-facing normals; these require triangle-level interpretation rather than being called stairs. No genuine staircase has been established by this restricted search.
